The river otter's afternoon

Field notes · 6 min read

Patient otters spend the morning grooming and the afternoon hunting at the river. Their fur is the densest of any animal — up to a million hairs per square inch — and it is this, not a layer of blubber, that keeps them warm through long stretches of activity in cold water.

A single forager, working a quiet bend, can take half its body weight in fish before dusk. They hunt by touch as much as by sight, sweeping the riverbed with sensitive whiskers until something darts, and the chase is on. The successful otter hauls its catch to a favourite rock, eats, grooms, and slips back under.

By evening the bank is quiet again. The otters have moved downstream to a holt dug into the roots of an old willow, where the day's hunting gives way to a long, companionable sleep.
